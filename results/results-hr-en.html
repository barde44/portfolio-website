Employee Performance Analysis — Python & R

Tools: Python (pandas, matplotlib, seaborn) | R (dplyr, ggplot2) Duration: ~4 hours

Business Problem

A company wants to analyze the performance distribution of its 1,470 employees to understand gaps between departments, identify satisfaction drivers, and detect atypical profiles (outliers).

"Which employees are salary outliers? Is there a bias in our evaluation system? Which departments are most satisfied?"

Project Objectives

  • Explore and clean the HR dataset with pandas
  • Calculate descriptive statistics (mean, median, quartiles, standard deviation)
  • Create 6 visualizations (histograms, boxplots, pie charts, heatmap)
  • Detect outliers using the 1.5 × IQR rule
  • Reproduce the analysis with R (dplyr + ggplot2)
  • Identify actionable insights for HR

Key Business Insights

Performance

  • Evaluation bias detected: 100% of employees have a rating of 3 or 4. This system does not differentiate performance.
  • 84.9% are rated "Excellent" (3), 15.1% "Outstanding" (4)

Salaries

  • R&D is the highest-paid department
  • 83 salary outliers (5.6%) earn more than $17,458/month
  • Median salary ($4,919) is much lower than the mean ($6,503) → skewed distribution

Satisfaction

  • 59.4% have high or very high satisfaction (3-4)
  • 40.6% have low or medium satisfaction (1-2) → significant improvement opportunity
  • 60.7% have good work-life balance (3-4)

HR Recommendations

  1. Revise the evaluation system: a system where 100% have a "good or excellent" rating doesn't help identify true talent.
  2. Analyze salary outliers: 83 employees earn 3× the median salary. Strategic positions?
  3. Satisfaction action plan: 40% dissatisfied is an alarm signal.
  4. Work-Life Balance: 39% have poor balance. Explore remote work and flexibility.
  5. Turnover risk: cross low satisfaction + low salary + poor balance to identify at-risk profiles.
