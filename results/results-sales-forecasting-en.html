Sales Forecasting -- Time Series

Tools: Python, statsmodels (ARIMA/SARIMAX), pandas, matplotlib, scikit-learn Duration: ~6 hours

Business Problem

A distributor wants to forecast weekly sales volumes to optimize inventory, plan promotions and manage logistics. With 208 weeks of data (2019-2022), the series shows a growing trend, a strong annual seasonality and a COVID shock in the first half of 2020. The goal is to compare several time series models to identify the one that best captures these dynamics.

"Which forecasting model best captures trend, seasonality, and exogenous shocks to predict sales 6 months ahead?"

Project Objectives

  • Explore and decompose the time series (trend, seasonality, residuals)
  • Test stationarity using the ADF test and determine the differencing order
  • Analyze ACF and PACF functions to guide parameter selection
  • Train and compare 4 models: ARIMA, SARIMA, Holt-Winters, Prophet-like
  • Evaluate performance on a 26-week test set (RMSE, MAE, MAPE)
  • Identify the best model and formulate operational recommendations

Series dynamics

  • The growing trend (+8 units/week) is well captured by Holt-Winters and ARIMA
  • Annual seasonality (52 weeks) is a key factor -- models without this component lose precision
  • The COVID shock (Q1-Q2 2020) remains a challenge for all history-based models
  • The quadratic acceleration of the trend favors adaptive models (exponential smoothing)

Limitations identified

  • No model drops below 20% MAPE -- promotional events and the COVID shock introduce uncaptured variability
  • SARIMA underperforms despite the seasonal component, possibly due to overfitting on the training set
  • The series is short (4 years) for robust estimation of annual seasonal parameters

Recommendations

  1. Deploy Holt-Winters as the operational forecasting model (best RMSE = 1,443)
  2. Recalibrate monthly to integrate the latest data points and maintain accuracy
  3. Add exogenous variables (promotions, holidays, weather) via SARIMAX to improve peak capture
  4. Monitor residuals continuously -- a structural change requires re-estimation
  5. Explore ML approaches (XGBoost, LSTM) if the series extends beyond 5 years
