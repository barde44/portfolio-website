Wine Profile Analysis — PCA (Principal Component Analysis)

Tools: Python (pandas, numpy, scikit-learn, matplotlib, seaborn) Duration: ~3 hours

Business Problem

A red wine producer wants to understand the chemical factors that influence wine quality. With 11 chemical variables measured on 1,599 samples, it is difficult to visually identify the axes of differentiation. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) reduces this complexity to reveal the key chemical profiles.

"Which chemical variables distinguish a high-quality wine? Can we reduce 11 dimensions to 2-3 interpretable axes?"

Project Objectives

  • Analyze the distributions and correlations of 11 chemical variables
  • Standardize the data (mandatory for PCA)
  • Apply PCA to identify principal components
  • Visualize 2D and 3D projections colored by quality
  • Interpret the loadings and the correlation circle (biplot)
  • Identify the chemical profiles of high vs low quality wines

Methodology

1. Data Exploration

  • 1,599 red wines, 12 columns
  • Quality distribution: 82.6% of wines rated 5 or 6
  • 0 missing values

2. Correlation Matrix

  • Notable correlations: fixed acidity / citric acid (+0.67), fixed acidity / density (+0.67), alcohol / density (-0.50)
  • Multicollinearity detected, justifying the PCA approach

3. Standardization and PCA

  • StandardScaler applied (mean = 0, standard deviation = 1)
  • Full PCA on all 11 components

Quality Wine Profile

  • High quality (7-8) wines stand out with high alcohol and low volatile acidity
  • Low quality (3-4) wines have higher volatile acidity (organoleptic defect)
  • PCA confirms that alcohol and volatile acidity are the best predictors of quality

Dimensionality Reduction

  • 11 variables reduced to 3 components without major information loss (~60%)
  • The scree plot shows a clear "elbow" after PC3
  • PCA enables interpretable visualization of an 11-dimensional space

Recommendations

  1. Prioritize alcohol and volatile acidity as quick quality indicators
  2. Use 3 components for any predictive model (73% feature reduction)
  3. Monitor chlorides (PC3) as a marker for potential defects
  4. Explore clustering (K-Means on components) to segment wine profiles
